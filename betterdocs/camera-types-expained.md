<h1>Camera Types Explained</h1>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">DIGITAL</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A digital camera does not use film to record images, it stores them electronically. Digital cameras come in all shapes and types; DSLR; Digital; Rangefinder;Compact and smart. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>DSLR</strong> - Digital Single Lens Reflex</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>DSLR</strong>:  image source: http://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/geoopt/slr.html   </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":44626,"width":"345px","height":"auto","sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-full is-resized"><img src="https://harryhilton.com/wp-content/uploads/LR/Cameras/Camera-work2.webp" alt="" class="wp-image-44626" style="width:345px;height:auto"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>DSLR cameras are directly related to film cameras in that they have, on the top, an image rendering glass pentaprism - a multi faceted glass optic that transfers the image by internally refracting (bouncing and bending in simple terms) it to the eye piece at the rear. The image passes through the lens on to the sensor which has a mirrore. The mirror allows the light and details to also pass to the sensor. When the shutter is pressed this mirror moves at high speed upwards to allow the sensor full access to the image that is then recorded onto storage media. The sensor is vertically lined up in the camera body whereas the reflective panel is at an angle to allow the image to be pointed upwards to the base of the glass prism.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Look at my Nikon D750 and D3000 pages for examples of this type of camera.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Best suited for:</strong> Enthusiasts, professionals, wildlife photographers, sports photographers, and those who want maximum lens choice and manual control.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Mirrorless</strong></h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Modern cameras are now more commonly of the Mirrorless design. The removal of the mirror block together with the pentaprism allows the cameras to be made both smaller and lighter.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":44629,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://harryhilton.com/wp-content/uploads/LR/Cameras/blob.webp" alt="" class="wp-image-44629"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In this type of camera the image is directed by the lens onto the sensor. The sensor is programmed by the camera into several modes - the most common are shown, usually on a top mounted dial, as "PASM" and represent P-Programmed to balance aperture and speed usually to parameters that may be adjustable (this does depend on the camera's complexity or otherwise; A - Aperture priority where the user will adjust the aperture to produce the photgraphic effect or output that they aim to create; S - Speed priority usually used for controlling movement effects or eliminating them as far as possible ( think of the photos of rivers and streams where the flow is smoothed by using slow speed or the use of high speed in sports where you wish to avoid blurring the image as much as possible) and finally M - Manual - this is down to you the photographer creating an image using your knowledge and understanding of cameras, composition and lighting to produce your picture. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Look at my Panasonic Lumix G80 page for this type of camera</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Best suited for:</strong> Travellers, enthusiasts, content creators, and photographers seeking high image quality in a lighter, more compact system.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Rangefinder</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A digital rangefinder camera is a modern take on what used to be a standard non SLR film camera.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Rangefinder cameras use a separate optical viewing system to focus and compose images rather than looking directly through the camera lens. Renowned for their compact design, quiet operation, and precise manual focusing, rangefinders have long been favoured by street and documentary photographers. Although less common today, they remain highly regarded by enthusiasts who appreciate their unique shooting experience and connection to the heritage of photography.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Look at my Panasonic Lumix GX9 page for this type of camera.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Best suited for:</strong> Photographers who enjoy a traditional hands-on approach, particularly for street, travel, and documentary photography where a compact, quiet, and unobtrusive camera is an advantage.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Compact.</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Compact cameras, often called point-and-shoot cameras, are designed to be small, lightweight, and easy to use. They feature a fixed lens and automatic settings, making them ideal for beginners, casual photographers, and travellers who want a simple camera without the complexity of interchangeable lenses.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Despite their small size, many modern compact cameras deliver excellent image quality and include useful features such as optical zoom, image stabilisation, and high-definition video recording. Premium compact models may also offer larger sensors and manual controls for photographers seeking greater creative flexibility.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Their biggest advantage is portability. Compact cameras easily fit into a pocket or small bag, making them ideal for holidays, city breaks, walking tours, and everyday photography. While they may not offer the versatility of a DSLR or mirrorless camera, they provide an excellent balance of image quality, convenience, and ease of use for travellers who want to capture great photos without carrying bulky equipment.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Best suited for:</strong> Travellers, beginners, families, and anyone looking for a lightweight camera that is easy to carry and simple to operate.</p>
